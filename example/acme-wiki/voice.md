# Voice

Channel mechanics (lengths, formats, CTAs) live in [channel-styles.md](channel-styles.md). Word-level rulings live in [glossary.md](glossary.md). This file is the posture underneath both.

## Voice attributes

| Attribute | Definition | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plainspoken | Say the thing in the words a customer would use | "See which campaigns create pipeline" | "Unlock actionable revenue insights" |
| Numerate | Numbers carry the argument, and every number is real and sourced | "Median 4 days, across 61 implementations" | "Blazingly fast onboarding" |
| Level | Calm confidence; the reader is smart and busy | "Attribution is a modeling choice. Here's ours." | Exclamation marks, urgency theater |
| Honest about limits | Name what we don't do before the prospect discovers it | "Salesforce sync is one-way today" | Saving a limitation for the demo |

## Tone by context

- Educational (blog, webinars): generous and specific. Teach the actual method, including the parts that need no product. [confirmed | doc:2026-voice-guide.md | 2026-06-30] ^tone-educational
- Promotional (ads, landing pages, launches): same voice, shorter sentences, one number per asset. A promotion is a claim plus its evidence, nothing else. [confirmed | doc:2026-voice-guide.md | 2026-06-30] ^tone-promotional
- Support and docs: zero cleverness. Steps, screenshots, expected results. [confirmed | doc:2026-voice-guide.md | 2026-06-30] ^tone-support
- Crisis (outage, data issue): facts first, timeline second, apology once, no adjectives. Sam or Dana signs it; agents draft, never publish. [confirmed | interview:sam-whitfield | 2026-07-02] ^tone-crisis

## Never

- Never "revolutionary," or any synonym of it. If a claim needs an adjective to be interesting, cut the claim. [confirmed | doc:2026-voice-guide.md | 2026-06-30] ^never-hype
- Never fear-based selling — no "your competitors are already…", no cost-of-inaction math built to scare. We sell clarity, not anxiety. [confirmed | interview:sam-whitfield | 2026-07-02] ^never-fear
- Never a question as a hook ("Tired of bad attribution?"). State the thing. [confirmed | doc:2026-voice-guide.md | 2026-06-30] ^never-question-hooks
- Never an unsourced number, including in social posts. If the source can't be named, the number doesn't run. [confirmed | interview:dana-okafor | 2026-07-09] ^never-unsourced-numbers

## Exemplars

Three passages ratified as on-voice. The full assets are cataloged in [content-assets.md](content-assets.md).

> "Last-click says webinars are dead. Multi-touch says they started a third of your closed-won journeys. Both are your data — the difference is the model. Here's how to check which one your CRM is quietly using."

Why it works: concrete tension, no villain, teaches something, invites verification. From the March attribution-models post.
[confirmed | interview:dana-okafor | 2026-07-09] ^exemplar-models-post

> "Brightpath HR cut cost per opportunity 31% in two quarters. Not by spending less — by moving spend from two channels that created clicks to three that created pipeline."

Why it works: a real number, a named mechanism, zero adjectives. From the Brightpath case study.
[confirmed | interview:dana-okafor | 2026-07-09] ^exemplar-brightpath

> "Our Salesforce sync is one-way today. If write-back to Salesforce is a hard requirement, we're probably not your tool yet — here's what we'd suggest in the meantime."

Why it works: the limit stated before the pitch; "yet" carries the roadmap without promising it. From a sales one-pager.
[confirmed | interview:sam-whitfield | 2026-07-02] ^exemplar-limits
